## 5. Two differentiated new practice candidates — full stem, solution and D6

These are NEW DELTA_C source candidates with **no canonical item_id allocated**. The public stem+answer+worked+remediation exist here once. Check whole cross-grade bank for semantic duplication before proposing new official ID. They do not enter protected RD1.

### CAND-P-M6-01 — Is continuity enough to guarantee a derivative?

**Question:** Let f(x)=ax+1 for x<1, and f(x)=x²+b for x≥1, with real a,b. Find a,b so that f has a derivative at x=1. Explain why checking continuity alone is insufficient.

**Why this method:** Differentiability implies continuity; first align function values at the join, then align the **two one-sided difference quotient limits** (slopes).

**Worked:** Left-hand limit at 1 is a+1; right-hand limit and f(1) are 1+b. Continuity requires b=a. The left derivative at 1 is a and right derivative is 2, thus a=2; with b=a we obtain b=2. Verify f(1)=3 on both sides and both one-sided slopes are 2. The only solution is (a,b)=(2,2).

**D6:** If you stop at b=a, try a=b=0: function values match at 1 but left slope 0 versus right slope 2. Revisit M6.2 corner and M5 continuity.

### CAND-P-M6-02 — A logarithm of a square root: which domain wins?

**Question:** Determine the real domain and derivative of F(x)=ln(√(4−x²)). Explain the status of x=±2.

**Why this method:** The outermost ln requires its **whole input strictly positive**. The square-root condition nonnegative is weaker and must be combined, not substituted.

**Worked:** Need √(4−x²)>0, equivalent 4−x²>0, hence −2<x<2. On this domain F(x)=½ln(4−x²), therefore

\[
F'(x)=\tfrac12\frac{-2x}{4-x^2}
=-\frac{x}{4-x^2},\qquad -2<x<2.
\]

At ±2 the square root is 0, so the logarithm is undefined; neither F nor F' exists there. Quick check at x=0 gives F'(0)=0, consistent with an even graph on its valid interval.

**D6:** If you include endpoints, you stopped at the square-root check and forgot ln0; if you use −x/√(4−x²), you differentiated only the root and ignored the log; if you use −2x/(4−x²), the factor ½ from ln√u was dropped.
