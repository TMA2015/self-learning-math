### CAND-P-M7-01 — Positive derivative on two branches does NOT imply global increasing

**Question:** Let f(x)=x+10 for x<0, and f(x)=x for x≥0. (a) Compute derivative on each open branch. (b) Is f strictly increasing on the whole real line? Explain why the sign test alone cannot settle this. (c) Check continuity at x=0.

**Worked solution and reason:** On x<0, f'=1; on x>0, f'=1. Yet f(−1)=9 whereas f(1)=1, and −1<1 while 9>1, so f is **not** increasing on all R. At 0 the left limit is 10; the right limit and f(0) are 0. The function has a **downward jump**, is discontinuous; derivative sign in separate intervals cannot cross the join. This tests an important transfer from M5→M6→M7 instead of replacing a G12 basic derivative-sign exercise. **D6:** if you answered “increasing everywhere because f'=1”, write the domain intervals and compare f(−1) vs f(1), then go back to M5 one-sided limits and M7.1.

### CAND-P-M7-02 — Continuous optimum vs integer fabrication constraint

**Question:** A lidless box is folded from 30×20 cm cardboard by cutting four squares of side x cm. A workshop can cut only **whole-centimetre** squares, so x must be an integer between 1 and 9. Compare the mathematically optimal continuous cut \`x*=(25−5√7)/3≈3.924\` cm to the permitted integer cuts. Prove which integer cut gives the maximum volume in the allowed set; explain why simply rounding a root without checking candidates would be unsafe in other problems.

**Worked solution and reason:** The physical model is \`V(x)=x(30−2x)(20−2x)\` for 0<x<10. \`V'(x)=4(3x²−50x+150)\` changes +→− at x*≈3.924 (the other root≈12.743 is outside domain). Thus for integers ≤3 the maximum is at x=3, and for integers ≥4 the maximum is at x=4; no other integer can beat one of those candidates. \`V(3)=3·24·14=1008\` cm³, \`V(4)=4·22·12=1056\` cm³. **Best feasible integer cut is x=4**, even though continuous x*≈3.924 with V*≈1056.306 cm³. **D6:** if selecting x* as a physical cut, underline “only whole centimetres”; if checking only x=4 with no proof, show continuous function monotonic intervals and compare x3/x4. In other models other nearest integers or extra constraints may matter; verify rather than blindly round.
